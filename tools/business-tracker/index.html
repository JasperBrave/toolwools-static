The ranking above is horizon-dependent. Acid Lab wins at 40 hours only because of its low buy-in; the Agency earns more per hour, so it overtakes Acid Lab once enough hours pass. How the tracker stays honest — formulas and data classes

The simulator computes only from numbers you enter. Effective net per play hour is your net $/h minus upkeep spread over the hours you play per day. Break-even, profit, ROI, crossover and time-to-goal all follow from those inputs, and every result card shows its inputs and formula on the same card — the “show your work” rule. Break-even shows “never” when daily net is zero or negative (p × h ≤ u), because the buy-in is then never repaid.

effPerHour   = p − u ÷ h                    (GTA$/h; p = net $/h, u = upkeep GTA$/day, h = hours/day)
dailyNet     = p × h − u                    (GTA$/day)
breakEvenDays = C ÷ dailyNet                 (days; only when dailyNet > 0, else "never")
breakEvenHours = breakEvenDays × h           (play hours)
profit(H)    = p × H − u × (H ÷ h) − C       (GTA$ at H play hours)
roi(H)       = profit(H) ÷ C                (shown as %)
crossover t* = (C_A − C_B) ÷ (eff_A − eff_B) (h; A vs B, eff = effective net $/h)
timeToGoal   = goal ÷ effPerHour             (h; only when effPerHour > 0, else "never")

GTA 6 business tracker questions

Buyable businesses, payout badges, the missing $/hour, and how the simulator works.

  • Are there buyable businesses in GTA 6?

    No. Rockstar has announced no player-owned businesses, and in the released game no business can be bought — the 12 named Leonida businesses are NPC-owned or visit-only, and the only property you can buy is a garage (8 for sale, $160,000 total). That may change with a patch, DLC or a future online mode, none of which has been announced.

  • Are these payouts verified?

    Only the structural facts are verified: the launch, the 12 named businesses, the garage total, the cash cap and the game's checklist counts. Every payout range on this page is guide-reported, not verified — each row carries its source, its date and a “Reported — not verified” badge. The GTA Online yardstick is labelled “GTA Online figures — not GTA 6” everywhere it appears, and the simulator's numbers are your own assumptions, not measured rates.

  • Why is there no $/hour for any GTA 6 method?

    Because no timed run has been published for any GTA 6 method. A $/hour rate needs a payout divided by a measured cycle time, and no source has published a timed run yet — PowerPyx's “~5 minutes” for bank vaults is an estimate, not a timed run. As soon as two independent timed runs exist for a method, its $/hour can be added with its sources.

  • What is the crossover point, and why does it matter?

    The crossover is the play-hours point where a business with a higher buy-in but a higher hourly rate overtakes a cheaper, slower one. On the GTA Online yardstick, the Agency ($2,830,000, $85,000/h) overtakes the Acid Lab ($750,000, $56,000/h) at 71.7 hours: (2,830,000 − 750,000) ÷ (85,000 − 56,000) = 71.7 h. Below 71.7 hours the Acid Lab is ahead ($1,490,000 vs $570,000 net at 40 h); above it, the Agency pulls away. The simulator computes the crossover for any two entries you enter.

  • Are Shark Cards worth it in GTA 6?

    There are no Shark Cards for GTA VI — none have been announced, and Rockstar has said there is no monetization inside the single-player campaign. The Shark Card figures on this page are GTA Online: the Megalodon pack (8,000,000 GTA$ for $99.99, 80,008 GTA$ per $1) is the best value, with an exact $100,000,000 GTA$ costing $1,249.88, or $1,299.87 in whole cards (13 × $99.99).

  • Can garages make money in GTA 6?

    No. Garages are the only property you can buy — 8 for sale, $160,000 total, $4,000–$40,000 each — but they store vehicles; they earn no money. Owning all 8 earns the “Little Boxes” trophy. Two individual prices are contested (Persistence Road $7,600 vs $9,500; Vela Rota $24,000 vs $30,000) and are flagged on this page, not resolved.

  • What happens to my money at the end of Chapter 1?

    It is wiped. Cash, checking and stash are lost at the Chapter 1 transition; only the $5,000 Family Savings deposit survives. Do not farm money in Chapter 1 — bank the $5,000 Family Savings deposit first, and start tracking from Chapter 2.

  • Who are the fences, and what do they pay?

    Five fences are named in the guides: Brian (Lockup, Key Lento), Donnie (Trailer, Key Lento), Dunk (Trailer, Port Gellhorn), Tyrone (La Perle, Vice City) and Yasmani (Little Cuba, Vice City). Brian and Donnie pay later via mailbox; Tyrone and Yasmani pay on the spot. What they pay per item is unpublished — no per-item price, cut or cooldown has been measured for any fence, so no fence rate appears on this page.

  • What is the Family Savings goal?

    Family Savings is the in-game savings goal. The first goal is $5,000 — and that deposit is the only money that survives the Chapter 1 wipe. The reported maximum goal is $2,000,000, from PowerPyx only, so it is labelled “Reported — not verified” on this page and used as the simulator's default goal.

  • How often are these figures re-checked?

    Weekly, until the verification gate clears: two independent sources confirming a per-job payout on screen, plus at least one timed $/hour run per method. Re-check triggers are any Rockstar Newswire post or Take-Two release naming a GTA VI online mode, businesses or Shark Cards, a bank-vault payout on screen, a Randy sale on screen, or a second source for any currently single-source figure. Every re-base is logged in the changelog below, with the “Last verified” date updated.

Business tracker terms, defined

Break-even
The play hours (and days, at your hours per day) after which a buy-in is fully repaid. Shows “never” when daily net is zero or negative.
Crossover
The hours at which a dearer, faster business overtakes a cheaper, slower one. Below it the cheap entry wins; above it the fast one does.
Payback hours
How many hours of play a business needs to repay its purchase price at its own net rate. Under twenty hours is a fast payback; over forty is a status purchase.
Reported — not verified
A figure published by a guide or community source with a source and date, but not confirmed by Rockstar or by a second independent on-screen run.
Yardstick
The GTA Online business table, kept clearly labelled as GTA Online figures — not GTA 6 — so the shape of buy-in vs hourly rate can be studied without pretending the numbers are GTA 6's.
Fence
A buyer of stolen goods in GTA 6. Five are named; none has a published per-item price, cut or cooldown.
