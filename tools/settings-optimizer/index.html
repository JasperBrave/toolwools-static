Tune it by hand in five passes

You do not need the optimizer to get 90% of the way there. This is the same order our benchmark desk uses.

  1. 01

    Set your resolution and upscaler before anything else

    Pick native resolution first, then enable DLSS / FSR / XeSS at Quality. Upscaling changes the cost of every other setting, so tuning before it is wasted work.

  2. 02

    Benchmark a repeatable scene, not a random drive

    Use the same 60-second route through a dense downtown block. Frame rate in an empty swamp tells you nothing about your worst case.

  3. 03

    Cut the three expensive options first

    Ray-traced reflections, volumetrics and shadows account for most of the gap between Ultra and High. Step each down one notch and re-run the same route.

  4. 04

    Max the free options back up

    Anisotropic filtering to 16x, textures to the highest tier your VRAM supports, ambient occlusion on. These cost single-digit percentages and carry most of the visual identity.

  5. 05

    Judge on 1% lows, not the average

    A 90 FPS average with 38 FPS lows feels worse than a locked 70. If your 1% lows are under half your average, you are VRAM-limited or CPU-limited, not settings-limited.

Inside the optimizer model

Per-setting costs are the measured average frame-rate delta from dropping one step (Ultra to High), not absolute frame rates. The optimizer runs the shared FPS model: it tries presets from Ultra down, picks the highest one that hits your target natively, then retries with DLSS Quality, then adds frame generation as a last resort. Frame-gen averages are interpolated frames, not measured.

presetFps   = (gpuScore / 100) x BASE_FPS[res] x PRESET_MULT[preset]
cpuCap      = (cpuScore / 100) x CPU_CAP_100[res]
avg         = min(presetFps x upscalerMult, cpuCap)
recommended = highest preset where avg >= target, native first
if none: retry with DLSS Quality (x1.30), then frame gen (x1.70 avg only)
low         = avg x 0.72 (0.62 when CPU-bound)
band        = [avg x 0.85, avg x 1.15]
  • Preset multipliers: Low x1.55, Medium x1.25, High 1.00, Very High x0.82, Ultra x0.68. BASE_FPS at High: 1080p 132, 1440p 102, ultrawide 1440p 84, 4K 62.
  • Per-setting cost order comes from the table above: ray-traced reflections 18-30%, volumetrics 8-14%, shadows 7-12%. Everything else is single digits until VRAM runs out.
  • VRAM shortage triggers a 25% avg FPS penalty and a 50% 1% low penalty, the driver spillover behaviour measured in Cyberpunk 2077 at over-budget VRAM configurations. Textures stay maxed only while the card clears the pool.
  • Frame generation multiplies avg by 1.7x (DLSS 3) or 1.6x (FSR 3); 1% lows are left unchanged because interpolated frames do not reduce real frame-time variance.

GTA 6 settings questions, answered

The tuning questions people actually search for now the game is out.

  • What are the best GTA 6 settings for 1080p 60 FPS?

    On a recommended-tier GPU, start from the High preset, turn ray-traced reflections off, drop volumetrics to Medium, and keep textures and 16x anisotropic filtering maxed. That combination historically returns 25-35% more frames than Ultra in RAGE-engine titles while looking near-identical in motion at 1080p.

  • Which GTA 6 setting costs the most FPS?

    Ray-traced reflections, by a wide margin, expect an 18-30% hit. Volumetric fog and shadow quality are next at roughly 8-14% and 7-12% respectively. Everything else is in single digits until you exhaust VRAM.

  • Should I use DLSS, FSR or XeSS in GTA 6?

    Use whichever your GPU supports natively: DLSS on RTX cards, FSR on Radeon, XeSS on Arc. At 1440p and 4K, Quality mode typically buys 30-45% more frames with a very small image-quality cost. At 1080p the upscaler has fewer pixels to work with, so prefer native plus a settings cut.

  • Is frame generation worth turning on?

    Only if your base frame rate is already above roughly 50 FPS. Frame generation multiplies smoothness but not responsiveness, enabling it at 30 FPS gives you a 60 FPS counter with 30 FPS input latency, which feels worse in a driving game than the lower number.

  • Do higher textures reduce FPS in GTA 6?

    Not directly. Texture quality is nearly free on average frame rate right up until you exceed your card's VRAM. Past that point the driver swaps textures over PCIe and your 1% lows collapse into visible stutter, which is why the VRAM calculator matters more than the settings menu here.

  • Are these settings recommendations final?

    No. Until Rockstar ships the PC build and its graphics menu, every figure on this page is modelled from RDR2 and GTA V PC, which share the RAGE engine and most of the same option names. We re-benchmark and republish within 48 hours of the PC release.

Graphics settings terms

Ray-traced reflections
The single most expensive option in RAGE titles, 18-30% of frame rate. Screen-space reflections look close in motion and cost almost nothing. Cut first.
Volumetric fog / clouds
Light scattered through haze and cloud volume. Vice City's humidity haze is heavy on volumetrics, 8-14%. Medium keeps the look and returns most of the frames.
1% low
Average of the slowest 1% of frames. Captures felt stutter. A 90 FPS average with 38 FPS lows feels worse than a locked 70.
Upscaler
DLSS, FSR or XeSS render internally at a lower resolution, then upscale. Quality mode typically buys 30-45% more frames at 1440p and 4K.
Frame generation
DLSS 3 / FSR 3 FG inserts a synthetic frame between real ones. Motion feels smoother; input latency and 1% lows are unchanged. Only worth it above roughly 50 FPS base.
Anisotropic filtering
Sharpens textures viewed at an angle. Effectively free on any GPU from the last decade, 1-2%. Set 16x and never lower it.
