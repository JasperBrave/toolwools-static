How to compare GTA 6 editions without overpaying

Five steps from platform to a defensible answer.

  1. 01

    Pick your platform

    PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series X|S both get Standard and Ultimate, digital-only.

  2. 02

    Say how you will play, not how much you want to spend

    A story-only player and a launch-week online grinder get opposite answers from the same table. The profile you choose sets the weights: currency and property matter enormously to one and nothing at all to the other.

  3. 03

    Set the uplift you would actually tolerate

    Instead of a price, you set a ceiling as a percentage over Standard. A 25% ceiling covers Ultimate, the top tier. Tiers above your ceiling are still shown, flagged, so you can see exactly what you are declining.

  4. 04

    Weight the categories you care about

    Currency, vehicles and property, cosmetics, early access. Selecting a category raises its weight on top of your profile.

    Read the added-value column, then the unknowns

How each edition bonus is scored and weighted

Each bonus carries an intrinsic usefulness out of ten and a confidence factor — confirmed bonuses count fully, reported ones at 0.8, and precedent-based ones at 0.55. Your player profile supplies a weight per category, and any category you explicitly prioritise gains a further 0.7. An edition's utility is the sum of its weighted, confidence-discounted bonuses. The score combines two things: how much usefulness the tier adds over Standard per point of uplift, and whether it actually contains the bonus categories you prioritised — then it penalises anything above the ceiling you set. Standard is scored on the fact that it is complete, not on bonuses it does not have.

utility = Σ (bonusValue × categoryWeight × confidenceFactor)
utilityDelta = utility(edition) − utility(standard)
efficiency = clamp(utilityDelta ÷ (upliftPercent ÷ 12), 0, 1.3)
coverage = prioritised categories present in tier ÷ prioritised categories chosen
score = clamp(0.30 + efficiency × 0.36 + coverage × 0.22, 0, 1) × (1 − budgetPenalty) × 100

Edition terminology used on this page

Uplift
The extra you pay for an edition, expressed as a percentage over the Standard edition in the same market. Currency-neutral, so it stays accurate everywhere.
Weighted usefulness
A bonus's intrinsic value multiplied by how much your player profile cares about that category and discounted by how firmly the bonus is established.
Confidence label
Confirmed means Rockstar has stated it. Reported means it appears in listings without full detail. Precedent means it is drawn from GTA V and RDR2 editions and may not apply to GTA 6.
Head start
Any bonus that only saves you time you would otherwise spend earning the same thing in-game — currency, starter vehicles, an early property.
Tier upgrade
An in-store purchase that converts an owned edition to a higher one at the price difference, rather than rebuying the game.
