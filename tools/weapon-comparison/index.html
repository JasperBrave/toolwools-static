What this page is — and what it is not

A comparison of the numbers the game shows, not a combat test. Every figure on this page belongs to exactly one of three data classes, and the three are never mixed in one row.

  • Two-source verified

    Two independent guides agree on the figure — always with the sources and dates beside it.

  • Reported — not verified

    One reputable guide says it, or it comes from a single transcription. Labelled as reported, never presented as confirmed.

  • Not published

    No source has published it — fire rate, exact damage, DPS, TTK, range in metres, reload times. Shown as “—”, never filled in.

Why there is no fire rate

The game shows four ratings and a fire mode — not a fire rate.

Every gun on an Ammu-Nation counter shows the same four numbers, each out of 10: Damage, Accuracy, Handling, Range. Pressing R3 opens a second page with magazine size, held ammo, fire style, brand and calibre. There is no fire-rate stat in the game UI, and no reputable source publishes one in RPM or out of 10.

The RPM numbers on some sites are invented. One page claims a “Tactical Carbine Mk II” at “750 RPM” while also claiming “GTA 6 Online launched in June 2026” — GTA 6 Online does not exist and the game launched 19 Nov 2026. Another prints exact figures like “Base Dmg: 32, RPM: 380, TTK 387 ms” with no source. A third lists “Damage 52, Range 42, Fire Rate 40, Accuracy 55” on a 1–100 scale that matches nothing the game shows. None of these is cited or used anywhere on this page. What the game does show is the fire mode — semi-auto, 3-round burst, full-auto, bolt-action, pump-action, break-action or single-shot — and that is the column this tool carries.

Ratings, not hit points

Damage 7.0 does not mean 7 HP.

“Damage” on this page is the game's own rating out of 10. No exact damage per shot, no DPS, no time-to-kill, no headshot multiplier, no damage falloff and no armour interaction has been published anywhere reputable — and no datamine of weapon files exists, because the game is console-only with no PC release. A rating bar compares the numbers the game shows; it does not measure combat performance. Treat a 9.6 launcher and a 1.5 small-game rifle as what they are: opposite ends of the same ten-point scale, not a kill-time prediction.

Attachments: what exists, and what is proven

Attachment categories exist for most guns. Per-attachment stat effects are published for almost none of them.

Attachments are bought and fitted at Ammu-Nation, only on weapons you own: optics and scopes, muzzles and suppressors, grips and foregrips, magazines, barrels and weapon lights. The modification menu shows how each attachment changes Damage, Accuracy, Handling and Range before you confirm the purchase — but published per-attachment numbers are thin, and one conflict is unresolved: one guide publishes exact suppressor prices and effects (Deever Tech Suppressor $720, Aftermath Suppressor $730, with ±0.2–0.5 effects on the Mediator), while another states no strong post-launch source publishes an exact suppressor price at all. Until a second source resolves that, this page publishes no suppressor price or effect as fact.

Two attachment effects are verified, and they are shown here as examples — not as a general table, because the Mediator's barrel does not prove anything about another gun:

  • Hawk & Little Mediator, 14" Carbine barrel — $320: Handling +0.4, Range −0.4 (Dexerto Mediator page, 22 Nov 2026).
  • Hawk & Little Mediator, Stiff Tip Micro sight — $65: Accuracy 8.0 (from 7.8), Handling 5.4 (from 5.6) (own PS5 recording, howtovideogame).

Finishes, palettes and wraps are cosmetic only. Extended magazines add capacity with no handling penalty where published (Mediator 24-round $110, 33-round $130; Duke 1922 extended magazine 8 → 36 rounds).

GTA 6 weapon questions

The best gun, the missing fire rate, and where the numbers come from.

  • What is the best weapon in GTA 6?

    There is no single best weapon — best depends on the job. By Damage rating, launchers top out at 9.2–9.6, the Fairview FF-50 is the hardest-hitting sniper at 8.5, pistols top out at 7.0 (Fairview Kalinga .50 and Scorpio Akulus), and all eight SMGs share Damage 4.2. The per-class strip above lists the best by Damage rating in each class. A ranking that weights Damage 40, Accuracy 30, Range 20 and Handling 10 is one honest way to rank; a single crown is opinion.

  • What is the fire rate in GTA 6?

    There is no fire-rate stat in GTA 6 — not in the game UI, and not in any reputable source, in RPM or out of 10. The game shows four ratings (Damage, Accuracy, Handling, Range) and a fire mode: semi-auto, 3-round burst, full-auto, bolt-action, pump-action, break-action or single-shot. The RPM numbers on some sites are invented, and this page does not use them.

  • How much damage does a gun do?

    Damage is a rating out of 10, not hit points. No exact damage per shot, DPS, time-to-kill, headshot multiplier, damage falloff or armour interaction has been published anywhere reputable, and no datamine of weapon files exists — the game is console-only with no PC release. This tool compares the ratings the game shows.

  • How many weapons are in GTA 6?

    53 base guns, plus 9 melee weapons, 4 explosives and 117–130 named unique variants, depending on which database you count (IGN, GTABase and PowerPyx disagree on the variant count). The in-game progress screen itself lists 53 entries under Weapons, Guns. This tool compares the 53 base guns, as of 24 Nov 2026 — variants, melee and throwables have no published stats, so they are not compared.

  • How do I unlock the Duke 1922?

    Reported, not verified: the Duke 1922 needs a BuckMe bank account, and its $1,050 price is marked disputed in the price table it comes from. Several guns are gated the same way — by a BuckMe account, by purchase count, by shooting-range gold, or by a mission. Each unlock note on this page carries its source.

  • Where do I buy sniper rifles?

    Reported: sniper rifles, machine guns and launchers are sold only at the six Expanded Ammu-Nation stores, out of 24 stores. The standard stores do not carry them. The Hauners H-AT rocket launcher is the most expensive gun in the game at $16,000 — though one guide says rockets cannot be bought at all, so its buyability is flagged on its row.

  • What is the cheapest gun in GTA 6?

    The Scorpio Nipper .38, at $550 in player guides — though IGN lists $495, so the price is shown as contested. It is a compact pistol with Damage 2.8, Accuracy 3.0, Handling 9.0 and Range 3.0, with an 8-round magazine. The most expensive is the Hauners H-AT at $16,000.

  • Where do the numbers on this page come from?

    From the game's own Ammu-Nation stat cards, as transcribed from the in-game menu by DayOne on 18 Nov 2026 and cross-checked against Dexerto's wiki, GTA6Info's weapons list, on-screen reads from launch streams (vAnother Level) and GrindStat's cross-check. Every figure on this page carries its source and date, and single-source figures carry a provisional badge. Rockstar has published no weapon stats itself. Stats may change with patches; every figure carries a “launch build, Nov 2026” stamp and the page is re-checked when the dataset grows.
