How your GTA 6 readiness grade is assembled

Five inputs, four sub-scores, one letter grade.

  1. 01

    Enter the whole build, not just the GPU

    GPU, CPU, RAM, free storage, drive type and download speed. The performance sub-score reuses the same engine as the FPS calculator — RTX 4090 = 100, 14900K / 7800X3D = 100 — and full storage marks need 212 GB free on an SSD at 111 Mbps or better.

  2. 02

    Compare your grade against the S – F band

    The weighted total maps to a letter grade. Verdict copy is deterministic per band, so the same rig always lands the same grade and two rigs one point apart never get contradictory advice.

  3. 03

    Fix the weakest link first, not the loudest one

    The lowest sub-score is named explicitly and linked to the calculator that diagnoses it — bottleneck, VRAM, storage planner or download time. Upgrading a strong sub-score barely moves the grade.

Sub-score weightings behind the grade

Four sub-scores are computed on a 0–100 scale and weighted. Performance uses the FPS engine at a reference 1080p High native target (60 fps = 100). VRAM compares card VRAM to the estimated 1080p High need with a 15% headroom bonus. Storage is (free GB / 212) × drive-type multiplier (NVMe 1.0, SATA 0.9, HDD 0.35). Network is (Mbps / 111) capped at 100.

readiness = perf × 0.55 + vram × 0.20 + storage × 0.15 + network × 0.10

perf     = clamp(fps_1080p_high_native / 60 × 100, 0, 100)
vram     = clamp((cardVram / vramNeeded − 0.5) / (1.15 − 0.5) × 100)
storage  = clamp(freeGB / 212 × 100) × driveMult
network  = clamp(mbps / 111 × 100)

grade  = S 92+ · A 82 · B 70 · C 55 · D 40 · F <40
  • Performance weight (0.55) reflects that framerate dwarfs every other launch complaint in the RDR2 and Cyberpunk 2077 post-launch subreddit analyses.
  • VRAM (0.20) is separated from performance because VRAM shortage causes stutter that raw avg-FPS numbers hide — it deserves its own signal.
  • Storage (0.15) applies an HDD multiplier of 0.35: mechanical drives cause open-world streaming stalls independent of free space.
  • Network (0.10) is the smallest weight because it only affects launch-week experience — but a stalled pre-load ruins launch night for a full-marks build.
  • Grade thresholds (S 92 / A 82 / B 70 / C 55 / D 40) were tuned so a mainstream 2024 mid-range build (RTX 3060 + Ryzen 5) with 16 GB RAM, an SSD and 150 Mbps scores 97 (grade S) under this model; B (70+) is the intended baseline for a smooth launch.

Readiness score questions

What the grade means and what to fix first.

  • Enter your GPU, CPU, RAM, free storage, drive type and internet speed. This tool composes the outputs of the FPS, VRAM, storage and download-time calculators into a single weighted 0–100 score with a letter grade. Anything B (70+) is a smooth launch; below D (40) means at least one component blocks a good experience.

  • 70 (B grade) is the practical floor for a comfortable launch — 60 fps at 1080p High, VRAM clears, 212 GB free on an SSD, and enough bandwidth to finish a 212 GB pre-load in about 4.2 hours. 82 (A) is a solid launch with one sub-score behind; 92+ (S) is the top band, tuned so a mainstream 2024 mid-range build (RTX 3060 + Ryzen 5) scores 97 here.

  • No — 16 GB clears this tool's baseline. Doubling to 32 GB only pays off if you multitask (Discord, browser, streaming) or if you plan to mod. Below 16 GB the FPS engine applies an 18% average and 40% 1% low penalty, so 8 GB builds tank the performance sub-score even with a strong GPU.

  • Yes, if 212+ GB of that is free. GTA 6's install is 212 GB before the day-one patch. On a 500 GB SSD with Windows and a couple of other games installed, expect this to be tight — plan for at least 212 GB of headroom on launch week.

  • This tool awards full network marks at 111 Mbps — that finishes a 212 GB download in about 4.2 hours. 50 Mbps finishes in about 9.4 hours; 25 Mbps in about 18.8 hours. On ADSL, start pre-loading the moment Rockstar opens the queue.

  • Mid-range gaming laptops (RTX 3060 mobile / RTX 4050+) with 16 GB RAM and an NVMe SSD typically land in B–C territory at 1080p Medium/High. Ultra-thin laptops with iGPU or MX-class GPUs fail the performance sub-score outright — no readiness score will save an under-spec dGPU.

  • At the PC launch, the game would still install and boot on hardware below Rockstar's floor, but performance is unpredictable — expect sub-30 fps averages, low-1% stutter and VRAM texture pop-in. This tool's F grade means you should treat pre-order as a placeholder and either upgrade the weakest link or wait for optimisation patches.

  • This tool uses ray tracing off as its reference. Enabling RT at launch will roughly halve the FPS sub-score on the same hardware — check the FPS calculator directly with your target preset to see the RT-on delta. VRAM headroom also shrinks by 1–2 GB with RT on.

Scoring terms

Readiness score
A single 0–100 number that combines performance, VRAM headroom, storage and internet into one weighted rating.
Weakest link
The lowest of your four sub-scores. Fixing it is always the highest-ROI upgrade because it's the one dragging your grade down.
Grade curve
S 92+, A 82, B 70, C 55, D 40, F <40. B or better means a smooth launch; D or worse means at least one component blocks a good experience.
Pre-load
Downloading a game before release day so it is ready to play at launch. GTA 6's console pre-load ran before the Nov 19, 2026 launch; there is no PC version yet, so the network sub-score measures whether your connection could finish a 212 GB download in time.
