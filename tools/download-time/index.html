How the download time is calculated

Total gigabytes × 8 = total gigabits. Divide by your effective speed (headline Mbps × efficiency) to get seconds, then convert to hours and minutes. The base-install and patch rows are calculated separately so you can see which part of the download dominates.

totalGB = baseGB + patchGB
effectiveMbps = mbps × (efficiency / 100)
seconds = totalGB × 8 × 1000 / effectiveMbps
finish = baseSeconds + patchSeconds
1 Gbps = 1000 Mbps; 1 GB = 8 Gb

Frequently asked questions

Honest answers to the questions people actually ask about downloading GTA 6 now that it has launched.

  • On a 100 Mbps connection with realistic 80% efficiency, a 212 GB base install plus 2.8 GB day-one patch (214.8 GB total) finishes in about 5 hours 58 minutes. On a 1 Gbps gigabit line it takes about 36 minutes; on a 25 Mbps DSL line it stretches to nearly 24 hours.

  • The install size is 212 GB (base) on PS5 and Xbox Series X|S, before the 2.8 GB day-one patch. There is no PC version of GTA 6. This tool defaults to a 212 GB base install and lets you dial it up.

  • Real-world downloads are gated by CDN throttling, TCP overhead, Wi-Fi loss, and disk write speed. PlayStation and Xbox CDNs typically deliver 70–85% of your ISP number. The efficiency slider models this — 80% is the realistic default.

  • T-Mobile and Verizon 5G home plans typically deliver 100–300 Mbps sustained, which finishes GTA 6 in about 1.5–4.5 hours. Latency isn't relevant for downloads; congestion during evening peak is — start the download in the early hours if you can.

  • Your total pull equals base install + day-one patch. On a Comcast 1.2 TB monthly cap, a 214.8 GB GTA 6 download uses about 18% of the month — plan accordingly if you have other streaming and updates queued.

  • Not the download itself — that's network-bound. But installing / unpacking to an HDD is measurably slower than to an NVMe SSD because Rockstar's installer writes and verifies as it goes. Expect a 10–20% total time penalty on HDDs.
