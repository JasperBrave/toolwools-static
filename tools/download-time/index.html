How the download time is calculated

Total gigabytes × 8 = total gigabits. Divide by your effective speed (headline Mbps × efficiency) to get seconds, then convert to hours and minutes. The base-install and patch rows are calculated separately so you can see which part of the download dominates.

totalGB = baseGB + patchGB
effectiveMbps = mbps × (efficiency / 100)
seconds = totalGB × 8 × 1000 / effectiveMbps
finish = baseSeconds + patchSeconds
1 Gbps = 1000 Mbps; 1 GB = 8 Gb

  • Speeds are measured in megabits per second (Mbps); file sizes in gigabytes (GB).
  • At 100 Mbps and 80% efficiency, 165 GB takes 4 hours 35 minutes; at 1 Gbps it takes under 30 minutes.

Frequently asked questions

Honest answers to the questions people actually ask before launch.

  • On a 100 Mbps connection with realistic 80% efficiency, a 150 GB base install plus 15 GB day-one patch (165 GB total) finishes in about 4 hours 35 minutes. On a 1 Gbps gigabit line it drops to under 30 minutes; on a 25 Mbps DSL line it stretches past 14 hours.

  • Rockstar has not published the final install size, but the studio's recent releases (RDR2 at 150 GB, GTA V Enhanced at 105 GB) plus higher-fidelity texture packs put the community estimate at roughly 150–200 GB before the day-one patch. This tool defaults to a 150 GB base install and lets you dial it up.

  • Real-world downloads are gated by CDN throttling, TCP overhead, Wi-Fi loss, and disk write speed. Steam and Rockstar CDNs typically deliver 70–85% of your ISP number. The efficiency slider models this — 80% is the realistic default.

  • Rockstar's recent pattern (GTA V Enhanced, RDR2) is a 48-hour pre-load window on console and 24–48 hours on PC. If you're on anything under 100 Mbps, start the pre-load the moment it opens or you'll miss launch night.

  • T-Mobile and Verizon 5G home plans typically deliver 100–300 Mbps sustained, which finishes GTA 6 in about 1.5–4.5 hours. Latency isn't relevant for downloads; congestion during evening peak is — start the pre-load in the early hours if you can.

  • Your total pull equals base install + day-one patch. On a Comcast 1.2 TB monthly cap, a 165 GB GTA 6 download uses about 14% of the month — plan accordingly if you have other streaming and updates queued.

  • Not the download itself — that's network-bound. But installing / unpacking to an HDD is measurably slower than to an NVMe SSD because Rockstar's installer writes and verifies as it goes. Expect a 10–20% total time penalty on HDDs.

  • Enter your speed and hit the connection preset that matches. If the estimated finish is longer than Rockstar's pre-load window (typically 24–48h before launch), start earlier — pause other downloads and hardwire your PC.
