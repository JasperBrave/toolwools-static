How GTA 6 FPS is estimated for your GPU and CPU

Five stages, from raw silicon score to a 1% low you can plan around.

  1. 01

    Pick your GPU + CPU

    Choose from the same 34-GPU catalog as the bottleneck calculator (21 CPUs). Scores are normalised so RTX 4090 = 100, i9-14900K = 100 and Ryzen 7 7800X3D = 98. We compute a GPU-bound FPS and a CPU cap separately, then take the minimum.

  2. 02

    Set resolution and preset

    1080p, 1440p, ultrawide 1440p or 4K, and Low → Ultra. Preset scales GPU load; resolution scales it further. CPU cap softens as resolution rises (CPU-bound at 1080p is normal, CPU-bound at 4K is rare).

  3. 03

    Layer an upscaler

    DLSS, FSR or XeSS shrinks the internal render resolution for a 1.25× to 1.75× GPU-side speedup. Quality mode costs the least image fidelity; Performance mode buys the most frames.

  4. 04

    Add frame generation, if your card has it

    DLSS 3 and FSR 3 insert a generated frame between two real ones, multiplying average fps by roughly 1.7×. Crucially it leaves 1% lows, and input latency, untouched, so we never let it move the low figure.

  5. 05

    Check the 1% low and the limiter before the average

    You get avg fps with a ±15% band, an honest 1% low, the limiter (GPU, CPU, VRAM or balanced) and a pass/fail against 30, 60, 120 and 144 fps. Short on VRAM and a stutter penalty is applied and named.

Inside the frame-rate model

GPU-bound FPS = (gpu.score / 100) × BASE_FPS[res] × PRESET_MULT[preset] × UPSCALER_MULT. CPU cap = (cpu.score / 100) × CPU_CAP_100[res]. Effective FPS = min(GPU-bound, CPU cap), reduced by a RAM penalty below 16 GB, further reduced by a VRAM stutter penalty when total needed exceeds the card. Frame generation multiplies avg only. 1% low = avg × 0.72 baseline, tightened when CPU-bound.

gpuBound    = (gpuScore / 100) × BASE_FPS[res] × PRESET_MULT[preset] × upscalerMult
cpuCap      = (cpuScore / 100) × CPU_CAP_100[res]
avg         = min(gpuBound, cpuCap)
avg         = ram < 16 ? avg × 0.82 : avg
if vramNeeded > cardVram: avg × 0.75, low × 0.50, limiter = VRAM
avg         = avg × frameGenMult             // FG lifts avg only
low         = avg × (cpuBound ? 0.62 : 0.72)  // 1% low
band        = [avg × 0.85, avg × 1.15]
  • GPU/CPU scores use the same 0–100 scale as the bottleneck calculator; RTX 4090 = 100, i9-14900K = 100 and Ryzen 7 7800X3D = 98.
  • BASE_FPS at 1440p High for a score-100 GPU + score-100 CPU is 102 fps, anchored to measured RDR2 4090 numbers and adjusted for GTA 6's denser Vice City streaming.
  • Preset multipliers (Low ×1.55, Medium ×1.25, High 1.00, Very High ×0.82, Ultra ×0.68) match Cyberpunk 2077 and RDR2 preset-scaling curves.
  • Upscaler multipliers are the gain over native at matched target resolution (DLSS-Q ×1.30, DLSS-B ×1.50, DLSS-P ×1.75, FSR-Q ×1.28, XeSS-Q ×1.25).
  • Frame generation multiplies avg by 1.7× (DLSS 3) or 1.6× (FSR 3); 1% lows are left unchanged because interpolated frames don't reduce real frame-time variance.
  • VRAM shortage triggers a 25% avg FPS penalty and a 50% 1% low penalty, the driver spillover behaviour measured in Cyberpunk 2077 at over-budget VRAM configurations.

Assumptions. FPS model calibrated from RDR2 launch-driver GPU scaling (Digital Foundry, Hardware Unboxed), Cyberpunk 2077 avg / 1% low ratios across presets, and measured DLSS 3 / FSR 3 upscaler + frame-generation multipliers on Alan Wake 2 and Cyberpunk. Reference: RTX 4090 + Ryzen 7 7800X3D at 1440p High = 102 fps. Uncertainty band ±15% versus launch-day drivers.

Frame rate, upscaling and frame-gen terms

Avg FPS
Mean frames per second across a benchmark run. The headline number, but it can hide stutter.
1% low
Average of the slowest 1% of frames. Captures felt stutter, a low 1% low means uneven pacing even when avg looks fine.
GPU-bound
GPU can't keep up with the CPU. Lowering resolution or preset lifts FPS. Typical at 1440p+ or high presets.
CPU-bound
CPU can't feed the GPU frames fast enough. Lowering resolution won't help. Typical at 1080p on strong GPUs.
Upscaler
DLSS, FSR or XeSS render internally at a lower resolution, then AI-upscale. Big FPS gain, minor image cost.
Frame generation
DLSS 3 / FSR 3 FG inserts a synthetic frame between real ones. Motion feels smoother; input latency and 1% lows are unchanged.
