How the GTA 6 money-per-hour math works

Payout minus buy-in, divided by the minutes a full cycle really takes.

  1. 01

    Net out the buy-in

    Crate loads, heist setup fees and supply runs come out of the payout before anything is ranked. A cargo sell that grosses $810K but costs $220K in crates is an $590K cycle, not an $810K one.

  2. 02

    Divide by real cycle minutes

    Cycle time includes setups, travel and the sell run, not just the finale timer. That is why an 11-minute payphone hit competes with a 95-minute heist on an hourly basis.

  3. 03

    Split per player where a crew is required

    Two-player content is also shown per participant, so a heist that pays more in total but needs a partner is compared honestly against solo work.

  4. 04

    Convert to time, not just money

    Enter a target and your current balance and the planner returns cycles, hours, in-game days and how many sessions of your usual length that means.

  5. 05

    Compare against ownership and against cash

    Business mode turns a purchase price into payback hours and profit at a chosen horizon. Shark Card mode converts each pack into GTA$ per real dollar and the grind hours it removes.

Grind plans for common GTA 6 savings goals

Four targets most players hit in their first hundred hours.

  • First million, fresh account

    No business, no heist board. Contract missions and payphone hits clear a million in roughly six focused hours with zero buy-in risk, faster in practice than saving for a business you cannot yet resupply.

  • Buying your first passive business

    A counterfeit lab at $1.75M repays itself in about fifteen play hours at its net rate, and only asks for nine active minutes per hour. That is the cheapest exit from pure mission grinding.

  • Saving for a heist-tier vehicle

    For a $4–5M target, mixing a nightclub's passive drip with heist finales beats stacking either alone: the club accrues while you run the board, so the two rates add instead of competing for the same hour.

  • Deciding whether to buy GTA$

    Set your realistic grind rate, then read the implied wage column. If a pack costs less per hour than the value you place on your own time, buying is rational. The table makes that trade explicit instead of moral.

Where these payout and payback numbers come from

Each method carries a projected gross payout, a buy-in cost and a full-cycle duration in minutes. Net rate = (payout − cost) ÷ minutes × 60. Businesses model gross production per play hour, supply cost per hour, and the active minutes per hour you must spend working them; payback hours = price ÷ (gross − cost). Shark Card value is a straight GTA$-per-USD ratio, then cross-multiplied against your chosen grind rate to express each pack as hours of play removed and an implied real-world hourly wage.

netPerCycle  = payout − cost
perHour      = round(netPerCycle ÷ minutes × 60)
perPlayer    = round(perHour ÷ players)

cycles       = ceil((goal − balance) ÷ netPerCycle)
hours        = (goal − balance) ÷ perHour
sessions     = ceil(hours ÷ sessionHours)

bizNetPerHour = grossPerHour − costPerHour
paybackHours  = price ÷ bizNetPerHour
profit(H)     = bizNetPerHour × H − price

cardPerUsd    = gtaDollars ÷ usd
hoursSaved    = gtaDollars ÷ grindPerHour
impliedWage   = usd ÷ hoursSaved
  • Cycle times are measured end-to-end, setup, travel, finale and sell, not from the mission timer.
  • Business production rates assume full upgrades, since unupgraded rates change the ranking order.
  • Shark Card prices track the standing US storefront; regional pricing changes the implied wage but not the ordering.
  • Payout projections are based on GTA Online methods. There are no GTA 6-specific methods or live values on this page yet.

GTA 6 money, business and Shark Card questions

Best method, fastest payback, and whether buying cash is ever rational.

  • What is the best money-making method in GTA 6?

    On a pure net-per-hour basis, the flagship heist finale on hard difficulty leads, because the hard-mode premium scales the payout without scaling the setup chain. But it needs a partner and a 95-minute block. If you play in shorter sessions or solo, payphone hits and contract chains produce a higher realised rate because you actually finish them.

  • How much money can you make per hour in GTA 6?

    A new account running contracts and payphone hits lands around $360K–500K per hour. A player with a nightclub and a warehouse running sell cycles alongside heist finales lands around $900K–1.4M per hour. The gap is almost entirely ownership: passive accrual stacks on top of whatever else you are doing.

  • Which business should you buy first in GTA 6?

    The one with the shortest payback that you can actually resupply. A counterfeit lab pays for itself in roughly fifteen hours and demands under ten active minutes per hour. A large cargo warehouse has the higher ceiling but needs twenty-plus active minutes an hour, so it only wins if you play long sessions.

  • Are Shark Cards worth it in GTA 6?

    As a value ratio, the largest pack always buys the most GTA$ per dollar. Small packs are roughly a third as efficient. Whether that is worth it depends on your grind rate: at $500K/hour, a $19.99 pack removes about two and a half hours of play, which is an implied wage of around eight dollars an hour. Above your own hourly value, buying is rational; below it, grind.

  • How long does it take to make $10 million in GTA 6?

    At an early-account rate of roughly $400K/hour, about twenty-five hours. With a nightclub and warehouse running and heist finales on cooldown, closer to eight to ten hours. Enter $10,000,000 as your target above and the planner returns the exact cycle and session count for the method you pick.

  • Do heists pay better than businesses in GTA 6?

    Per hour of active play, heists pay more. Per hour of your life, businesses often win, because a nightclub or lab accrues while you do something else. The correct answer for most players is both: own passive production, spend your attention on heists.

  • Does the payout change with more players in a heist?

    Total take rises with crew size but is split, so per-player earnings usually fall unless the extra players cut the clock significantly. The calculator shows both total and per-player rates so you can see when adding a fourth member is actually costing you money.

  • Why does this calculator use net payouts instead of the headline number?

    Because headline numbers are marketing. A crate sell that grosses $810K costs $220K to stock, and a heist finale carries a setup fee. Ranking by gross puts high-buy-in content at the top of every guide even when its net rate is mediocre. Net-per-hour is the only ranking that survives contact with a real session.

  • How accurate are these GTA 6 payouts now that the game has launched?

    They are projections, not datamines. Payouts are extrapolated from GTA Online's twelve-year payout curve and scaled for the launch-economy reset Rockstar has signalled. Treat the ordering as reliable and the absolute values as ±30%. No live GTA 6 payouts are shown on this page yet. All nine methods are GTA Online methods, shown as the closest available analogues.

  • Does this include single-player money, or only online?

    The method list is built for the shared online economy, where buy-ins, cooldowns and crew splits exist. Story-mode heists have no supply costs or resupply loops, so their payouts are not directly comparable and are excluded rather than mixed in.

GTA economy terms, defined

Net per hour
Payout minus buy-in, divided by full cycle time. The only rate that lets a short zero-cost mission be compared fairly against a long high-buy-in heist.
Payback hours
How many hours of play a business needs to repay its purchase price at its own net rate. Under twenty hours is a fast payback; over forty is a status purchase.
Active share
The percentage of each production hour you must spend physically working a business, resupplying, driving, selling. Low active share is what makes income feel passive.
Buy-in
Money spent before a payout arrives: crate purchases, supply runs, heist setup fees. Ignoring it is the most common error in money guides.
Implied wage
The real-money cost of a Shark Card divided by the play hours it removes. Converts a purchase decision into a number you can compare to your own time.
Cooldown
The forced wait before a payout source can be repeated. Cooldowns cap the realistic rate of high-value content and are why mixing methods beats spamming one.
